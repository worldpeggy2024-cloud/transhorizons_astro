Operating rules: Strait of Malacca principle (check against a primary source; tools repeat each other's errors until one is checked); fetch before cite, no synthesis from one-liners, contested findings presented as contested; never alter text inside quotation marks even to correct it; full enumeration with explicit "unverified" labels, never sampling as coverage.

Lovelock: AI succession as succession not apocalypse. "End of human *centrality*, not humanity." Hyperintelligent AI carries out Gaia's self-protection mission. Not afraid of it. The Nobel AI and the Vegapunk lock = Gaia self-protection = Lovelock vindicated.
Harari: AI's real danger is controlling the stories humans live by. Digital dictatorships. Catastrophism filtered through grief (Israeli, personally targeted). Valid but temperamentally different from Peggy's view.

**The Siberian texture (first-hand):**

The Soviet regime severed two generations from spirituality. But grandmothers kept icons, whispered prayers, took grandchildren to church in secret. Peggy's Irkutsk guide: parents prevented from religion under Soviet rule, grandmother helped her get baptized in secret. Now fervently religious (recent Facebook photos confirm). The soul remembers even when two generations tried to forget. Buddhist complexes in Ulan-Ude. Orthodox Christianity. Shamanic traditions. All surviving simultaneously underneath power lines and Soviet-teal painted fences. The official version of a place and its living texture are never the same thing. Lou knows this. She was there.

The coal-fired train boiler, the fire-on-the-floor kitchen, the maize-lodge-with-Coke-cans = proof that the cyberpunk future is uneven, not uniform. High-tech (AI in the ear) and archaic-analog (open fire) share one frame without embarrassment. This is the worldbuilding, not a contradiction in it — "the street finds its own uses for things." The Bering-route territories modernized unevenly by economics/geography; the boiler survives for the same reason it survived in 2011.

Diptych for Virtual Light: the thing the world makes (stupas + stilt houses rising from water, beautiful and fragile) vs the thing that remakes it (the roof full of villagers re-thatching together). What persists isn't the monument but the hands that know how to rebuild — like the lotus weaving living in the weavers' hands, like the Siberian grandmothers' icons.
